\section{Entropic regularization}
\label{sec:entropic-regularization}

\subsection{Volume-constrained problem}
\label{sec:entropic-volume}

A practical route to maximizing $\dualenergy(\weight)$ discretizes the domain into a set of $m$ samples $\{\xx_k\}$ with masses $\{\nu_k\}$ and replaces the hard assignment by a soft coupling $\coupling \!\in\! \R^{n \times m}_{\ge 0}$, whose entry $\coupling_{ik}$ is the mass transported from sample $\xx_k$ to site $\qq_i$.
Its \emph{entropy} is
\begin{equation}
  \entropy(\coupling) = -\!\sum\nolimits_{i,k} \coupling_{ik} \bigl( \log \coupling_{ik} - 1 \bigr) .
  \label{eq:entropy}
\end{equation}
Adding this entropy term, weighted by a regularization parameter $\eps \!>\! 0$, to the transport cost gives the regularized problem
\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:entropic-ot}
  \begin{alignat}{2}
    & \min\nolimits_{\coupling} \quad && \sum\nolimits_{i,k} \coupling_{ik} \, \norm{\xx_k - \qq_i}^2 \; - \; \eps \, \entropy(\coupling) \label{eq:entropic-ot-obj} \\
    & \text{s.t.} \quad && \sum\nolimits_k \coupling_{ik} = \pvol_i \ \ \forall i , \label{eq:entropic-ot-row} \\
    & && \sum\nolimits_i \coupling_{ik} = \nu_k \ \ \forall k . \label{eq:entropic-ot-col}
  \end{alignat}
\end{subequations}
with the row and column marginals enforcing the target volumes and sample masses.
The optimal coupling has the Gibbs form
\begin{equation}
  \coupling_{ik} = \exp\!\Bigl( \tfrac{1}{\eps} \bigl( \weight_i + \samplepot_k - \norm{\xx_k - \qq_i}^2 \bigr) \Bigr) ,
  \label{eq:gibbs}
\end{equation}
written entirely in log space through a site potential and a sample potential $\samplepot_k$.
Crucially, the site potential is exactly the power-diagram weight $\weight_i$: the entropic problem is a regularized power diagram in which the weights play the role of the site scalings, recovering the exact weights as $\eps \!\to\! 0$.
Enforcing the marginals then amounts to the alternating \emph{Sinkhorn} updates, which in log space read
\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:sinkhorn}
  \begin{empheq}[left={\empheqlbrace\,}]{align}
    \weight_i &\leftarrow \eps \log \pvol_i - \eps \log \sum\nolimits_k \exp\!\bigl( \tfrac{1}{\eps} ( \samplepot_k - \norm{\xx_k - \qq_i}^2 ) \bigr) , \label{eq:sinkhorn-u} \\
    \samplepot_k &\leftarrow \eps \log \nu_k - \eps \log \sum\nolimits_i \exp\!\bigl( \tfrac{1}{\eps} ( \weight_i - \norm{\xx_k - \qq_i}^2 ) \bigr) , \label{eq:sinkhorn-v}
  \end{empheq}
\end{subequations}
where the site update is a soft-max counterpart of the weight step \eqref{eq:newton-step} and converges to the entropic optimal transport plan.
The regularization produces a \emph{soft} power diagram: the hard minimum of the power distance in \eqref{eq:power-cell} is replaced by a soft minimum, so the assignment $\coupling_{ik}$ acts as a smooth density (indicator) of each cell, as discussed by \citet{Qu2022PowerPIC}.

\subsection{Volume and centroid constraints together}
\label{sec:entropic-joint}

Discretizing the domain and adding the entropy \eqref{eq:entropy} to the joint problem of \S\ref{sec:contributions} yields a regularized problem, now with \emph{two} site-side potentials --- the weight $\weight_i$ for the volume constraint and the shift $\shift_i$ for the centroid constraint.
The optimal coupling takes the log-space Gibbs form
\begin{equation}
  \coupling_{ik} = \exp\!\Bigl( \tfrac{1}{\eps} \bigl( \weight_i + 2\,\shift_i\tr (\xx_k - \tcentroid_i) + \samplepot_k - \norm{\xx_k - \qq_i}^2 \bigr) \Bigr) ,
  \label{eq:joint-gibbs}
\end{equation}
in which the weight and shift are exactly the dual variables of the two constraints.
The sample potentials $\samplepot_k$ are updated by the same soft-max column step as in \eqref{eq:sinkhorn-v}.
The site potentials, however, no longer have a closed-form update, because a single site $i$ now carries two coupled constraints --- one scalar (volume) and one vector (centroid).
We therefore solve, for each site independently, the small strictly convex program
\begin{equation}
  \min\nolimits_{\weight_i, \shift_i} \ \eps \sum\nolimits_k \coupling_{ik}(\weight_i, \shift_i)
    - \weight_i \, \pvol_i - 2\,\shift_i\tr \tcentroid_i \, \pvol_i ,
  \label{eq:joint-site-solve}
\end{equation}
whose gradient enforces the row marginal and centroid marginal jointly and whose Hessian is the local covariance of the samples weighted by $\coupling_{ik}$ --- positive definite, so each site solve is a well-posed Newton step.
Alternating these joint site solves with the column update converges, and unlike the unregularized joint problem the regularized objective is convex in the potentials $(\weight, \shift, \samplepot)$, since the entropic dual is a log-sum-exp --- a convex function --- of variables entering affinely through the exponent of \eqref{eq:joint-gibbs}.
