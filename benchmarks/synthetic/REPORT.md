# Synthetic benchmark, 2D: H vs. G with fixed weights (2026-11-12)

All 600 2D cases (`README.md`: 20 sizes n = 10…10000, noise 0/1/2,
uniform/Voronoi volumes, seeds 1–5), each solved twice:

- **H** (`run_synthetic.py`): shifts and weights, from the sites q = c̄
  (`MakeJointStart`, s = 0). Results in `results/ours.csv`.
- **G** (`run_synthetic_g.py`): shifts only, from zero shifts, with sites
  q = c̄ and fixed weights w = w_Y − |Y − c̄|², so that the solution is the
  synthetic diagram (Y, w_Y) itself. Results in `results/oursG.csv`.

Both stop when the volume and shift residuals are ≤ 1e-8 (per cell,
relative; `DualSolver`). G does not constrain the volumes; its volume
residual is measured on the final cells. Logs: `run_H_2d.log`,
`run_G_2d.log`. Tables regenerated by `report.py`.

**Timings are ballpark only.** Other experiments ran on the machine at the
same time: load average 2.5–6.9 during H and 3.4–13.3 during G (`load`
column of the CSVs).

## Outcomes of G

The start of G is the power diagram of (c̄, w). Each case gets one of:

- **converged**;
- **invalid start**: the start has empty cells (counted exactly by
  `benchBourne`, `startEmptyCells`). An empty cell has zero gradient and
  zero Hessian, and the solver accepts no step that keeps a cell empty, so
  G cannot take a single step. This is a property of the data and of the
  fixed weights, not of the solve.
- **failed**: valid start, but the solve did not converge.

## Accuracy of the converged solves

| solver | cases | iterations | max volume residual | max shift residual | generator error / h | diagram mismatch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H | 598 | 6 – 386 | 1.1e-10 – 1.0e-08 | 9.3e-12 – 9.9e-09 | 1.7e-07 – 7.8 | 0 – 3.3e-05 |
| G | 523 | 4 – 31 | 2.9e-13 – 1.0e-07 | 9.3e-14 – 9.9e-09 | 8.0e-13 – 1.4e-06 | 0 – 1.0e-06 |

- **diagram mismatch**: fraction of the square (1000 × 1000 grid) assigned
  to a different cell than by the synthetic diagram. Both solvers recover
  the diagram: at most 3.3e-5 for H and 1e-6 for G (one grid cell).
- **generator error** (max |p − Y| / h): for G it is measured directly, since
  with fixed weights the solution is unique. For H it is measured after
  removing a dilation about the centroid. It still reaches 0.1–7.8 h in 24
  converged H cases whose diagram mismatch is ~1e-6. So H's generators are
  not unique beyond that dilation. Presumably these are the extra
  null-space modes noted in `src/core/dualSolver.h` (one per extra
  connected component of the interior facets, e.g. a cell with a single
  interior facet); not checked case by case. For H, judge recovery by the
  diagram mismatch, not the generator error.

## Failures

**H** (2): `noise2-uniform-6952-5` and `noise2-uniform-10000-1`. Both
stopped at the iteration cap (4 attempts, 400 iterations) with residuals
2.9e-8 / 2.0e-8 and 1.2e-8 / 6.3e-9, i.e. within 3× of the tolerance.
G has an invalid start on both.

**G with a valid start** (15). All stopped with "damping failed" after
23–37 iterations, with generator errors of 0.3–1.4 h. H converged on all
15. They are 13 uniform cases (noise 0: 3, noise 1: 9, noise 2: 1) and 2
Voronoi ones (`noise1-voronoi-785-5`, `noise2-voronoi-4833-3`). In the two
traced
(`noise2-uniform-379-2`, `noise2-voronoi-4833-3`), one cell shrinks
monotonically to zero volume (1e-27 and 1e-23 of V̄) while G barely
increases. G at the stall is below G at the solution (1.285e-3 vs. 1.493e-3,
6.0796e-5 vs. 6.0955e-5), so the stall is not a local maximum: the ascent
direction passes through diagrams with an empty cell, which the step
acceptance rejects.

When G converges, it takes about 9 iterations at every n. H's iteration

an H solve. But the G medians are only over the cases where G converged
(22 of 30 at n = 10000).

## Key findings

1. **H recovers the diagram in 598/600 cases**, and the two misses end within
   3× of the tolerance. Its start, a volume solve, never has empty cells.
2. **G, given the true weights, fails on 77/600 cases.**
   - 62 are invalid starts: the diagram of (c̄, w) loses cells. This
     happens only with uniform volumes, at noise 1–2, and becomes certain at
     noise 2 from n ≈ 1100.
   - 15 more stall when a cell collapses during the solve.
   - With Voronoi volumes the weights are nearly uniform, and G converges on
     298/300.
3. **When G does converge, it is faster** (about 9 iterations, independent
   of n) **and more accurate in the generators** (unique solution).
4. Knowing the weights doesn't make the inverse problem easier for a
   shift-only solver: fixing them removes the freedom that keeps every cell
   non-empty along the path. That is the case for solving for weights and
   shifts together (H).
